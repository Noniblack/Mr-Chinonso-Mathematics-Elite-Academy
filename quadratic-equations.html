FOUNDATION

What is a quadratic equation?

A quadratic equation is an equation that can be written in the form ax² + bx + c = 0, where a ≠ 0. The highest power of the variable is 2. Mastery begins with recognising this structure and understanding how the coefficients affect the equation.

METHODS OF SOLUTION

Four major routes to solving quadratic equations.

A strong learner should understand the reasoning behind each method and recognise when a method is useful.

1. Factorisation

Rewrite a suitable quadratic as a product of factors, then use the zero-product principle to determine the roots.

Best developed through pattern recognition and careful verification.
2. Completing the Square

Transform the quadratic into a perfect-square form. This method also provides a bridge to vertex form and graphical interpretation.

Especially useful for connecting algebraic and graphical ideas.
3. Quadratic Formula

For ax² + bx + c = 0, the quadratic formula provides a general method for finding the roots.

Useful when factorisation is difficult or unsuitable.
4. Graphical Solution

Interpret solutions through the points where a quadratic graph meets the x-axis, while connecting algebraic roots with graphical features.

Develops interpretation rather than relying only on symbolic manipulation.

FREQUENT QUESTIONS

Quadratic Equations FAQ

What makes an equation quadratic?

Its highest power of the variable is 2, and it can be represented in the standard form ax² + bx + c = 0 with a ≠ 0.

Which method should I use to solve a quadratic equation?

It depends on the structure of the equation. A strong learner should be able to recognise suitable methods and verify the resulting solutions.

Is completing the square useful beyond solving equations?

Yes. It provides an important connection between quadratic equations, perfect squares, vertex form and graphical interpretation.

Can quadratic equations be used in examination preparation?

Yes. The mathematical concepts can be developed through progressively challenging practice and then applied to the relevant requirements of an examination pathway.
