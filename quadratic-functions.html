What Is a Quadratic Function?

A quadratic function is a function whose highest power of the variable is 2. In standard form it can be written as:

f(x) = ax² + bx + c,   a ≠ 0

The graph of a quadratic function is a parabola. Understanding the algebraic form and the graph together is one of the most important skills in quadratic mathematics.

Essential Graph Features

  • Vertex: the turning point of the parabola.
  • Axis of symmetry: the vertical line through the vertex.
  • x-intercepts: points where the graph meets the x-axis; these correspond to roots.
  • y-intercept: the point where the graph meets the y-axis.
  • Maximum or minimum point: determined by whether the parabola opens downward or upward.
  • Opening: the sign of a determines whether the parabola opens upward or downward.

From Quadratic Equations to Quadratic Functions

Quadratic equations and quadratic functions are closely connected, but they are not the same idea. An equation asks for values that make a statement true, while a function describes a relationship between an input and an output.

On the graph, solving a quadratic equation can correspond to locating the x-values where the parabola meets the x-axis. This connection allows students to move naturally between algebraic and graphical methods.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Confusing the roots with the y-intercept.
  • Plotting table values inaccurately and then drawing a distorted parabola.
  • Forgetting that the sign of a determines the direction in which the parabola opens.
  • Reading a maximum or minimum value without identifying the corresponding coordinate.
  • Confusing the axis of symmetry with the x-axis.
  • Giving graph-based answers without checking the scale of the axes.

Frequently Asked Questions

  • What is a quadratic function? A function that can be written in the form f(x) = ax² + bx + c, where a ≠ 0.
  • What is the graph of a quadratic function called? It is called a parabola.
  • What are roots on a quadratic graph? They are the x-values where the graph intersects the x-axis.
  • What is the vertex? It is the turning point of the parabola and may represent a maximum or minimum point.
  • Can a graph solve an equation? Yes. Appropriate intersections or x-intercepts can provide graphical solutions.
