FOUNDATIONAL THEORY

Lesson 1: Angles and right-angled triangles

Trigonometry studies relationships between angles and sides of triangles. In a right-angled triangle, one angle is exactly 90°.

Angles

Angles may be measured in degrees. A full turn is 360°, a straight angle is 180°, and a right angle is 90°.

Angles in a triangle add up to 180°.

Identify the sides

Hypotenuse: opposite the right angle and the longest side.

Opposite: across from the chosen angle.

Adjacent: next to the chosen angle, but not the hypotenuse.

Pythagoras' theorem

For a right-angled triangle with perpendicular sides \(a,b\) and hypotenuse \(c\):

a² + b² = c²

Use this relationship only for right-angled triangles.

Important: Opposite and adjacent depend on which acute angle you choose. The hypotenuse does not change.
CORE FORMULAS

Lesson 2: The three trigonometric ratios

For an acute angle θ in a right-angled triangle, remember SOH–CAH–TOA.

Sine — SOH

sin θ = Opposite / Hypotenuse

Use sine when the opposite side and hypotenuse are involved.

Cosine — CAH

cos θ = Adjacent / Hypotenuse

Use cosine when the adjacent side and hypotenuse are involved.

Tangent — TOA

tan θ = Opposite / Adjacent

Use tangent when the opposite and adjacent sides are involved.

NON-RIGHT-ANGLED TRIANGLES

Lesson 3: Sine rule and cosine rule

These rules can be used for triangles that are not right-angled. Match each side with the angle directly opposite it.

The sine rule

a / sin A = b / sin B = c / sin C

Use it when you know an opposite side-angle pair and another side or angle needed to form a proportion.

Example: In a triangle, A = 30°, B = 60° and side a = 8 cm. Find side b.

  1. Write the sine rule: b / sin 60° = 8 / sin 30°.
  2. Rearrange: b = 8 × sin 60° / sin 30°.
  3. Substitute: b = 8 × 0.8660 / 0.5.
  4. Answer: b ≈ 13.86 cm.

The cosine rule

c² = a² + b² − 2ab cos C

Here, angle C is between sides a and b, and side c is opposite angle C.

Example: Two sides of a triangle are 5 cm and 7 cm, and their included angle is 60°. Find the third side c.

  1. Substitute into the cosine rule: c² = 5² + 7² − 2(5)(7)cos 60°.
  2. Since cos 60° = 0.5, c² = 25 + 49 − 35 = 39.
  3. Take the positive square root: c = √39.
  4. Answer: c ≈ 6.24 cm.
Choosing between the rules: the sine rule uses known opposite side-angle pairs. The cosine rule is especially useful when two sides and their included angle are known, or when all three sides are known and an angle is required.
REAL-LIFE APPLICATIONS

Lesson 4: Bearings, elevation and depression

Bearings

A three-figure bearing is measured clockwise from north. For example, 065° is written with three figures.

Draw a north line at the starting point and measure the direction clockwise from it.

Angle of elevation

The angle between the horizontal line through an observer and the line of sight to an object above that horizontal.

Common examples include viewing the top of a building or a tree.

Angle of depression

The angle between the horizontal line through an observer and the line of sight to an object below that horizontal.

Draw the horizontal carefully before identifying the angle.

Worked example: Angle of elevation

A student stands 20 m from the foot of a vertical tower. The angle of elevation to the top is 35°. Find the height of the tower, assuming level ground and ignoring the student's eye height.

  1. Identify the opposite side as the tower height h and the adjacent side as 20 m.
  2. Use tangent: tan 35° = h / 20.
  3. Rearrange: h = 20 tan 35°.
  4. Answer: h ≈ 14.00 m.
IMPORTANT RELATIONSHIPS

Lesson 5: Trigonometric identities

Pythagorean identity

sin² θ + cos² θ = 1

This identity holds for all angles where the trigonometric functions are defined.

Tangent identity

tan θ = sin θ / cos θ

Use this where cos θ ≠ 0.

Complementary angles

sin(90° − θ) = cos θ

For an acute angle, the sine of its complement equals the cosine of the angle.

STEP-BY-STEP SOLUTIONS

Worked examples

Example 1: Find an unknown side

A right-angled triangle has an angle of 30° and a hypotenuse of 12 cm. Find the side opposite the 30° angle.

  1. We know the opposite side and hypotenuse are involved, so use sine.
  2. sin 30° = opposite / 12.
  3. Opposite = 12 × sin 30° = 12 × 0.5.
  4. Answer: 6 cm.

Example 2: Find an angle

In a right-angled triangle, the opposite side to angle θ is 6 cm and the adjacent side is 8 cm. Find θ to one decimal place.

  1. Use tangent because the opposite and adjacent sides are known.
  2. tan θ = 6 / 8 = 0.75.
  3. θ = tan⁻¹(0.75).
  4. Answer: θ ≈ 36.9°.

Example 3: Find the hypotenuse

A right-angled triangle has perpendicular sides of 9 cm and 12 cm. Find its hypotenuse.

  1. Apply Pythagoras' theorem: c² = 9² + 12².
  2. c² = 81 + 144 = 225.
  3. c = √225.
  4. Answer: 15 cm.
YOUR TURN

Guided examination practice

Attempt each question first. Open the solution only after you have tried it.

Question 1 — Right-angled triangle

A right-angled triangle has an angle of 40° and an adjacent side of 10 cm. Find the hypotenuse to 2 decimal places.

Solution: cos 40° = 10 / h, so h = 10 / cos 40° ≈ 13.05 cm.

Question 2 — Angle of elevation

A point on level ground is 15 m from a building. The angle of elevation to its top is 28°. Estimate the building's height.

Solution: tan 28° = h / 15, so h = 15 tan 28° ≈ 7.98 m.

Question 3 — Sine rule

In triangle ABC, A = 30°, B = 45° and side a = 10 cm. Find side b to 2 decimal places.

Solution: b / sin 45° = 10 / sin 30°, so b = 10 sin 45° / sin 30° ≈ 14.14 cm.

Question 4 — Cosine rule

Two sides of a triangle are 6 cm and 8 cm. The included angle is 60°. Find the third side to 2 decimal places.

Solution: c² = 6² + 8² − 2(6)(8)cos 60° = 52. Thus c = √52 ≈ 7.21 cm.

Question 5 — Bearings

A point B is due east of point A. State the three-figure bearing of B from A.

Solution: Bearings are measured clockwise from north. Due east is 090°.
