Lessons, Formulas and Worked Examples

Work through the lessons in sequence. Show your calculations and label your graph axes.

Lesson 1: Cartesian Coordinates and Ordered Pairs

The Cartesian plane is formed by two perpendicular number lines: the horizontal x-axis and the vertical y-axis. They meet at the origin, \((0,0)\).

A point is represented by an ordered pair \((x,y)\). Move horizontally first, then vertically. The order matters: \((3,2)\) is not the same point as \((2,3)\).

The four quadrants

  • Quadrant I: \(x\) positive, \(y\) positive.
  • Quadrant II: \(x\) negative, \(y\) positive.
  • Quadrant III: both coordinates negative.
  • Quadrant IV: \(x\) positive, \(y\) negative.

Plotting checklist

  1. Draw perpendicular axes.
  2. Choose a consistent scale.
  3. Label both axes and the origin.
  4. Plot each ordered pair accurately.
  5. Label the points when required.

Worked example

Plot \(A(2,3)\), \(B(-2,1)\) and \(C(-1,-2)\).

\(A\) is in Quadrant I, \(B\) is in Quadrant II, and \(C\) is in Quadrant III. Plot each point by moving along the x-axis direction first and then to its y-coordinate.

Lesson 2: Tables of Values and Plotting Functions

A function rule tells you how to calculate \(y\) from a chosen value of \(x\). A table organises the pairs before you plot them.

Calculate the value of \(y\) when \(x=3\) for \(y=2x+1\).

\(y=2(3)+1=7\).

Graphing rule: Plot points accurately, use a consistent scale, and join them according to the nature of the function. A linear function produces a straight line; a quadratic function produces a curve.

Lesson 3: Linear Graphs, Gradients and Intercepts

A linear function has the form:

\(y=mx+c\)
  • \(m\) is the gradient or slope.
  • \(c\) is the y-intercept, where the graph crosses the y-axis.
\(\displaystyle m=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}\)

The gradient measures vertical change divided by horizontal change. A positive gradient rises from left to right; a negative gradient falls.

Worked example

Find the gradient of the line through \(A(1,3)\) and \(B(4,9)\).

\(\displaystyle m=\frac{9-3}{4-1}=\frac{6}{3}=2\).

The gradient is \(2\).

Worked graph: \(y=2x+1\)

The graph has a positive gradient of 2 and a y-intercept of 1.

Lesson 4: Solving Simultaneous Equations Graphically

The solution of two simultaneous linear equations is the point where their graphs intersect. The coordinates of the intersection satisfy both equations.

Worked example

Solve graphically:

\(y=x+1\) and \(y=-x+5\).

At the intersection, the y-values are equal: \(x+1=-x+5\).

\(2x=4\), so \(x=2\).

\(y=2+1=3\).

Solution: \((2,3)\).

To solve graphically, plot both lines on the same axes and read the coordinates of their intersection.

Parallel distinct lines have no intersection, so the equations have no common solution. Coincident lines share all their points, so the equations have infinitely many solutions.

Lesson 5: Quadratic Graphs, Roots and Turning Points

A quadratic function has the form:

\(y=ax^2+bx+c,\quad a\ne0\)
  • The graph is a parabola.
  • If \(a\) is positive, it opens upwards.
  • If \(a\) is negative, it opens downwards.
  • Roots are the x-values where the graph crosses or touches the x-axis.
  • The y-intercept is \(c\).
  • The turning point is the vertex of the parabola.
\(\displaystyle x_{\text{vertex}}=-\frac{b}{2a}\)

Worked example: \(y=x^2-4\)

Find the roots and turning point.

At the roots, \(y=0\): \(x^2-4=0\).

\((x-2)(x+2)=0\), giving \(x=-2\) or \(x=2\).

The roots are \((-2,0)\) and \((2,0)\).

The symmetry axis is \(x=0\). Substituting \(x=0\) gives \(y=-4\), so the turning point is \((0,-4)\).

The curve opens upwards and is symmetric about the y-axis.

Lesson 6: Reciprocal and Other Nonlinear Graphs

Nonlinear functions do not generally produce straight-line graphs. Their shapes and features depend on the function rule.

Reciprocal function

\(y=\frac{1}{x},\quad x\ne0\)
  • Undefined at \(x=0\).
  • Has two separate branches.
  • Approaches, but does not meet, the axes.

Cubic function

\(y=x^3\)
  • Passes through the origin.
  • Is an odd function.
  • Increases from left to right.

Worked example

Find \(y\) when \(x=-2,-1,1,2\) for \(y=1/x\).

\(x\)-2-112
\(y\)-0.5-110.5

Plot the four points. Draw smooth branches in Quadrants I and III. Do not connect the branches across \(x=0\).

Other nonlinear graphs may include exponential, logarithmic and higher-degree polynomial functions, depending on the syllabus.

Lesson 7: Graphs of Trigonometric Functions

Trigonometric graphs describe repeating relationships between an angle and a trigonometric ratio. They are important for studying periodic behaviour and solving suitable equations.

Angle units: The graphs below use degrees, from \(-360^\circ\) to \(360^\circ\). In radians, one full revolution is \(2\pi\). Make sure you use the angle unit specified in the question.

A. Sine graph: \(y=\sin x\)

Range: \(-1\le y\le1\). Period: \(360^\circ\) or \(2\pi\) radians.

The sine curve starts at zero, reaches a maximum of 1, returns to zero, reaches a minimum of -1, and repeats.

B. Cosine graph: \(y=\cos x\)

Range: \(-1\le y\le1\). Period: \(360^\circ\) or \(2\pi\) radians.

The cosine curve starts at 1 when \(x=0^\circ\), reaches zero at \(90^\circ\), reaches -1 at \(180^\circ\), and repeats every \(360^\circ\).

C. Tangent graph: \(y=\tan x\)

Period: \(180^\circ\) or \(\pi\) radians. Range: all real numbers.

Tangent is undefined at \(90^\circ+180^\circ k\), where \(k\) is an integer. The dashed lines indicate vertical asymptotes, not points on the graph.

Worked example: finding values from a sine graph

Find \(\sin 30^\circ\), \(\sin 90^\circ\) and \(\sin 180^\circ\).

From the standard sine values or the graph: \(\sin30^\circ=\frac12\), \(\sin90^\circ=1\), and \(\sin180^\circ=0\).

Key features to identify

  • Amplitude: for \(y=a\sin x\) or \(y=a\cos x\), the amplitude is \(|a|\).
  • Period: the horizontal length of one complete cycle.
  • Range: the set of possible y-values.
  • Intercepts: points where the graph crosses an axis.
  • Symmetry: sine and tangent are odd functions; cosine is even.
  • Asymptotes: tangent has vertical asymptotes where cosine is zero.
Important distinction: Sine and cosine are defined for every real angle. Tangent is undefined where \(\cos x=0\). Never join separate tangent branches across an asymptote.

Lesson 8: Transformations of Functions

Transformations change the position, size or orientation of a graph. The exact transformations covered in an examination depend on its syllabus.

\(y=a\sin(bx)\), \(\qquad y=a\cos(bx)\)
  • \(|a|\) determines the amplitude.
  • For nonzero \(b\), the period in degrees is \(360^\circ/|b|\).
  • The period in radians is \(2\pi/|b|\).
  • A negative \(a\) reflects the graph across the x-axis.
  • \(y=f(x)+d\) shifts a graph vertically by \(d\).
  • \(y=f(x-h)\) shifts a graph horizontally by \(h\).

Worked example

Find the amplitude and period of \(y=3\sin(2x)\).

Amplitude \(=|3|=3\).

Period \(=360^\circ/2=180^\circ\), or \(\pi\) radians.

The graph ranges from -3 to 3 and completes one cycle in \(180^\circ\).

Phase shifts, combined transformations and more advanced trigonometric modelling may be included in international or pre-university courses.

Lesson 9: Solving Equations and Modelling Real Situations

Graphs can represent the relationship between quantities such as distance and time, costs and output, or the height of a periodic wave.

Worked example: graphical solution

Suppose a graph of \(y=x^2-4\) is drawn. To solve \(x^2-4=0\) graphically, locate where the curve crosses the x-axis.

The intersections occur at \(x=-2\) and \(x=2\).

For \(x^2-4=5\), draw or consider the horizontal line \(y=5\). Its intersections with the parabola give \(x=\pm3\).

Interpreting a real-world graph: read the axis labels and units first, identify the scale, state values with units, and distinguish interpolation (within known data) from extrapolation (beyond the data).

Examination Practice with Worked Solutions

Attempt each question before opening its solution.

Question 1 — Gradient of a straight line

Find the gradient of the line passing through \((2,5)\) and \((6,13)\).

Solution: \(\displaystyle m=\frac{13-5}{6-2}=\frac84=2\).

Question 2 — Table of values

Find \(y\) when \(x=-2,0,2\) for \(y=x^2+1\).

Solution: the values are \(5,1,5\), respectively. The corresponding points are \((-2,5)\), \((0,1)\) and \((2,5)\).

Question 3 — Quadratic roots

Find the roots of \(y=x^2-9\).

Solution: set \(y=0\). Then \(x^2-9=0\), so \((x-3)(x+3)=0\). Therefore, \(x=-3\) or \(x=3\).

Question 4 — Simultaneous equations

Solve \(y=2x\) and \(y=-x+6\).

Solution: at the intersection, \(2x=-x+6\). Hence \(3x=6\), \(x=2\), and \(y=4\). The intersection is \((2,4)\).

Question 5 — Sine graph

State the maximum value and period of \(y=\sin x\), where \(x\) is measured in degrees.

Solution: the maximum value is \(1\), and the period is \(360^\circ\).

Question 6 — Tangent graph

State the period of \(y=\tan x\) and identify its first positive vertical asymptote.

Solution: the period is \(180^\circ\). The first positive vertical asymptote is at \(x=90^\circ\).

Question 7 — Function transformation

Find the amplitude and period of \(y=2\cos(3x)\), with \(x\) in degrees.

Solution: amplitude \(=2\). Period \(=360^\circ/3=120^\circ\).

Question 8 — Reciprocal function

Why is \(x=0\) excluded from the domain of \(y=1/x\)?

Solution: substituting \(x=0\) would require division by zero, which is undefined.
